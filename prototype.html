Pre-assessment assistant
Guides, gathers, never diagnoses

What this replaces

Today, international patients email imaging back and forth before anyone knows if the trip to London makes sense. This assistant runs the first structured conversation, then hands a clean summary to the clinical team for the real decision.

Design rules

  • Never a verdict. Output is a structured referral summary, not a yes/no.
  • Mirrors the clinic's own language - "suitability before salesmanship".
  • Every answer becomes data the team sees before the consultation.
  • Type or tap. Typed answers are parsed into the same structured fields - try "I tore my meniscus skiing".
Under the hood - how this works in production

The chips are scripted so the demo behaves identically every time, and the text box runs a deliberately small, deterministic keyword parser so you can watch typed answers land in the same structured fields - each parsed message shows what was recorded. In production that parsing runs on an LLM constrained to a clinician-approved question set: it can rephrase, translate, and follow up naturally, but the clinical content is frozen.

Guardrails: no diagnosis, no probability of success, no pricing negotiation. Anything outside scope routes to a human. Multilingual by default, which matters for an international patient base.

Why this is the core idea

NanoACi 100 stands or falls on follow-up. Patients fly home, and paper questionnaires at fixed timepoints get lost. A conversational check-in collects the same validated outcome measures (KOOS-style) with far higher completion, and feeds the registry automatically.

Design rules

  • Frozen protocol. Questions map 1:1 to the registry's outcome measures at the pre-registered timepoints.
  • Red flags escalate to humans immediately - the AI never manages a complication. Try typing "my calf feels swollen" at any question, then open tab 3 to see what the team receives.
  • Shared record: London team and the patient's local physio see the same trajectory.
Under the hood - how this works in production

Check-ins are scheduled to the study's frozen follow-up plan (e.g. 6 weeks, 3, 6, 12 months) and delivered over WhatsApp or SMS, where international patients actually respond. Instrument items are delivered verbatim, in validated translations only - the LLM handles the conversation around them, never the instrument text, and scoring uses the instrument's own arithmetic.

Safety triage: a deterministic rule layer screens every reply for red-flag terms (fever, calf pain, wound issues) before anything else happens. The text box in this demo runs a miniature version of that exact rule layer - a typed red-flag term interrupts whatever question was being asked. A hit sends the patient an instant clinician-approved safety-net message first, including local emergency guidance, then pages the team with full context - a red flag is never left waiting on a human's response time.

Under the hood - how this works in production

The registry is the product's centre of gravity. Every conversation in tabs 1 and 2 writes structured rows here, so the dataset for the technique paper → outcomes series → comparative work pipeline builds itself as care happens, including complications and non-responders - which is exactly what the published NanoACi 100 methodology promises to capture.

Outlier detection is statistics, not AI: deviation from the cohort's own distribution at each frozen timepoint. The AI's job is upstream - getting complete, honest, timely patient-reported data in.

The flywheel - evidence in, clients out

Each turn of the loop makes the next one cheaper. One shared data layer underneath.

The front door is a tool

The candidacy check embeds on nanoaci.com and surfaces through the MSK Doctors network. A visitor doesn't read a brochure, they have a conversation, and they leave as a qualified, imaging-ready enquiry in their own language. Acquisition without ad spend, in the clinic's own "suitability first" voice.

Their own AI joins the loop

Professor Lee already built MAI Motion, phone-camera movement analysis. The design reserves a slot: a check-in can hand the patient to a MAI Motion sit-to-stand or squat test at each timepoint, so the registry can hold objective biomechanics next to patient-reported scores. If MAI Motion fills that slot, we believe nobody else in cartilage repair can currently show that pairing.

Every patient recruits their physio

The shared recovery record gives the patient's local physio a working line to the London team. They watch the recovery succeed first-hand, then send the next patient. A professionals view with clear referral criteria turns that goodwill into a channel.

Under the hood - the rules that keep this credible

Health advertising in the UK is tightly regulated, so the growth engine publishes only what the registry can substantiate, including non-responders. Patient stories need explicit consent. Outcome data is special-category data under UK GDPR, and every platform connection above has to respect that from day one. Those constraints aren't a brake on the 360 offer, they're why it works for a brand built on "keep the science".
